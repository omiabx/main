# The SpongeBob RICH Theory: Voiceover Script

Runtime target: about 6:30. Roughly 1,000 spoken words at a relaxed YouTube pace, before edit gaps and clip beats.

### 01
There's an episode of SpongeBob where SpongeBob becomes a millionaire. I'm not exaggerating for the intro. Somebody pays him over a million dollars for a pearl, and for about a day, he's the richest guy in Bikini Bottom. Then he loses all of it. Which, fine. It's a cartoon. Lesson learned, back to the grill.

### 02
Except I kept pulling on that, and it got weird. Because when you track SpongeBob's money across the whole show, he doesn't look like a broke fry cook. He looks like a guy who gets rich by accident, again and again, and then tosses it like a gum wrapper. And at the end of this, there's a number I really don't think Mr. Krabs wants anyone to work out.

### 03
Okay. Start with what he actually gets paid. Season one, Squeaky Boots. SpongeBob asks Mr. Krabs for his paycheck. And Mr. Krabs hands him a pair of old boots instead. And SpongeBob loves them.

### 04
Season three. Mr. Krabs sells SpongeBob to the Flying Dutchman. Sells him. For sixty-two cents. Even Squidward thinks that's too far. Then in season nine, Krabs fires him to save a nickel. So if you're keeping score, SpongeBob's career earnings are one pair of boots, and his market value is under a dollar.

### 05
Now look at how this guy lives. He owns a pineapple with more than one floor. He's got a pet snail he feeds every single day. And he's been enrolled at Mrs. Puff's Boating School for basically the entire show. In Mrs. Puff, You're Fired, we find out he's failed the test one million, two hundred fifty-eight thousand and fifty-six times.

### 06
Someone is paying for those lessons. A man who gets paid in boots is somehow funding the longest education in the history of the ocean. There's a fan theory that his parents are loaded, and they bought him the house. Could be. But I think the show tells us where the money comes from. Twice.

### 07
Season two. Patty Hype. The Krusty Krab is dead empty, so SpongeBob paints Krabby Patties in six different colors and calls them Pretty Patties. Mr. Krabs won't sell them. So SpongeBob sets up his own little stand, and the line gets so long that fans have actually counted it. Forty-six thousand, eight hundred and fifty-three people.

### 08
And my favorite detail in the entire show is what happens next. SpongeBob and Patrick are making so much money that they don't know what to do with it. So they open a second stand. It says Free Money. They're handing the cash back to customers... so the customers can buy more Pretty Patties.

### 09
That's an infinite money glitch. A sponge found an infinite money glitch in season two. Mr. Krabs sees it on the news, and he trades SpongeBob the key to the entire Krusty Krab, just to get that stand. For a little while, SpongeBob SquarePants owned the Krusty Krab.

### 10
Then, season six. Porous Pockets. SpongeBob is using a giant pearl as a volleyball. Somebody notices, buys it off him, and pays more than a million dollars. That's the whole business plan. He was playing volleyball with his retirement fund.

### 11
He turns his pineapple into a gold mansion. People he's never met move in. He hands money to anyone who asks, and the second it runs out, every single one of them leaves. The only person who warned him was Patrick. Patrick!

### 12
So put those two next to each other. Twice, the show gives SpongeBob a fortune. Twice, it ends the same way. The money's gone, and he's back at the grill, happy. And look, the guy can clearly make money. He's done it two separate times, both by accident. What he can't do is keep it. And I'm honestly not sure he's even trying.

### 13
Which brings me to the number. In the first movie, from two thousand four, SpongeBob has a wall of Employee of the Month awards. Three hundred and seventy-four of them. In a row. That's a monthly award. Three hundred seventy-four months is thirty-one years. Without missing a single month.

### 14
So let's pay him. And I'm not going to be generous. I'm using the US federal minimum wage, seven twenty-five an hour, which hasn't gone up since two thousand nine. Forty hours a week, every week, for thirty-one years. That comes out to about four hundred and seventy thousand dollars. And that's before overtime. This sponge does not go home.

### 15
Now compare that to what he actually got. A pair of boots. So, technically, the guy who owes SpongeBob the most money in Bikini Bottom is the richest crab in the sea. SpongeBob is sitting on a half-a-million-dollar IOU. And the one time he asked about it... he got boots.

### 16
Now, I did try to break this theory, because it felt too clean. And the obvious problem is, both times, he ends up with nothing. So right now, today, is SpongeBob rich? No. He's got a snail, a pineapple, and a boating school bill I don't even want to think about.

### 17
But I keep coming back to that Free Money stand. Everybody in that town is chasing money. Krabs sold his best employee for sixty-two cents. SpongeBob found an infinite money glitch, and he used it to give the money away so people could keep eating. He could be the richest person in Bikini Bottom whenever he feels like it. He's proven that twice. He'd just rather flip patties.

### 18
So, Mr. Krabs. If you're watching this. Four hundred and seventy thousand dollars. Plus interest. Or, you know. Boots.
