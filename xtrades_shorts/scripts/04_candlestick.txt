Every candle on a stock chart holds four prices, which are the open, the high, the low, and the close.
The thick part is the body, and it runs from the open to the close.
If the close is above the open, the candle is usually green, and buyers won.
If the close is below the open, it is usually red, and sellers won.
The thin lines are called wicks, and they mark the highest and lowest prices that traded.
Say a stock opens at $100, climbs to $106, drops to $97, and closes at $104.
You get a green body from $100 to $104, with wicks reaching up to $106 and down to $97.
A long top wick means buyers pushed the price up but could not hold it.
The trap is that some apps flip the colors or use hollow candles, so check the legend first.
