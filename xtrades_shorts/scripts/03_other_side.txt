The SEC says more than 90% of regular investors' orders that fill right away do not go to a stock exchange.
They go to a small group of trading firms called wholesalers.
Every stock has a bid, which is the most someone will pay, and an ask, which is the least someone will sell for.
Say a stock has a bid of $10.00 and an ask of $10.02.
The wholesaler can sell to you near $10.02, buy from someone else near $10.00, and keep the gap.
Then it pays your broker a small fee for sending your order, which is called payment for order flow.
That fee is one way an app can charge you zero commission.
By law, you have to get the best public price or better.
The trap is using a market order on a stock that barely trades, because the gap can be huge, and you pay all of it.
A limit order lets you set the most you will pay.
