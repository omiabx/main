## part1.mp3 · Hook, map, the problem, electricity  (1050 characters)

```
A new phone has more switches inside it than there are people on Earth.
You've never seen any of them. Each one is thousands of times thinner than a hair. And every single one is made from sand.
Today, Bob finds out how.

This is stop seven. Bob has finally left the desert, and the fog is gone.
Welcome to the Foundry. This is where computers stop being an idea, and start being something you can hold.

Back in the desert, everything came down to switches. On is one, off is zero.
But every switch Bob used, he flipped himself. When he sent his face across the canyon, he was flipping lamps until the sun went down.
A computer has to flip billions of switches every second, with no hands. So Bob needs a switch that electricity can flip, instead of a finger.

Think of electricity like water in a pipe. When nothing is in the way, it flows all the way around, and the lamp at the end lights up.
A switch is just a gap you can open and close. Close the gap, and electricity flows. That's a one. Open the gap, and nothing gets through. That's a zero.
```

## part2.mp3 · Materials, the transistor, switches flipping switches  (936 characters)

```
Some materials let electricity through really easily, like copper. That's why wires are made of it.
Others, like glass, block it almost completely.
And then there's silicon. Silicon comes from sand, and it's somewhere in the middle. It's picky. With the right tricks, you get to decide when it lets electricity through.

So, put a tiny piece of silicon between two wires. On its own, it blocks the electricity. The gap is open.
Now add a third wire on top. Send a little electricity into that one, and the silicon underneath starts letting electricity through. The gap closes.
Turn it off, and the gap opens again.
That's a transistor. A switch with no lever. Electricity flips it.

This is the part that matters. The electricity coming out of one transistor can flip the next one. And that one can flip the next.
Switches flipping switches, and nobody touches anything. Pretty much everything a computer does is built on that one idea.
```
