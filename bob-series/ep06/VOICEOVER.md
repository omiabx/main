## part1.mp3 · Hook, recap, the problem, what sound is  (1031 characters)

```
Right now, you're listening to numbers.
Your phone got a long list of them, and it's turning them back into my voice, more than forty thousand numbers every second.
Stick around, because later you'll hear what happens when it doesn't get enough of them.

This is stop six. The last stop in the desert.
Bob has already sent his friend words across this canyon. Last time, he even sent a picture of his face.
And now his friend wants to hear his voice.

So Bob does the obvious thing. He shouts.
But the canyon is wide, and the wind is loud. By the time his shout gets to the other side, there's nothing left of it.
He's going to have to send his voice the same way he sent everything else. As numbers. And for that, we first need to know what sound actually is.

Sound is air shaking.
When you talk, your throat pushes the air back and forth, really fast. That air bumps into the air next to it, which bumps the air next to that, all the way to someone's ear.
Their ear feels the air shaking, and their brain hears it as your voice.
```

## part2.mp3 · Drawing the shake, measuring, the speaker  (928 characters)

```
A long time ago, people figured out how to draw that shaking.
You talk into a big horn. At the other end, a tiny needle shakes along with your voice and scratches a line onto a moving strip of paper.
What comes out is a wiggly line. A big wiggle means a loud sound. A fast wiggle means a high sound.

Bob draws his voice the same way. But a wiggly line still isn't numbers.
So he grabs a ruler. He measures how high the line is right here. Then a tiny bit later, he measures again. And again. And again.
Every measurement is just one number. Put them in a row, and that row of numbers is his voice.

He flashes the numbers across the canyon, one at a time.
On the other side, his friend has a speaker. It's basically a paper circle that can move in and out, and each number tells it how far to push.
Push, pull, push, pull, thousands of times. The air starts shaking the same way Bob's throat shook it. And his friend hears Bob.
```

## part3.mp3 · Too few measurements, enough, precision  (672 characters)

```
There's a catch. How often do you measure?
If Bob only measures now and then, he misses most of the wiggles. The speaker gets a rough, blocky copy, and it sounds awful.
Here's the first line of this video again, measured only two thousand times a second.

And here it is measured forty-four thousand times a second, which is what music on your phone usually uses.

That's fast enough to catch every wiggle your ears can hear.
Each measurement needs to be precise too. Eight lamps can only count to two hundred and fifty-five, so the line comes out a bit jagged.
For music, computers use sixteen lamps for every measurement. That's enough to count past sixty-five thousand.
```
