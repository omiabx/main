## 2. Angle options

**Angle A: "Two Bikinis" (recommended)**
Two thirds lore, one third real history. First, build the case that Bikini Bottom has the full machinery of a religion: a god who lives above the clouds, people who kneel and pray to him, a soul collector, a hell with a sin list, a heaven gag, and a holiday imported by a missionary from Texas. But no church, and no one who goes to one. Then the turn: the town is named after a real place that **did** have a church, where people were asked after a Sunday service to leave for "the good of mankind". This lands the channel's dark twist with facts that survive the comments, and it uses public-domain U.S. Navy Operation Crossroads footage.

**Angle B: "The Gospel of Neptune"**
Pure in-universe lore, structured like a religious-studies breakdown: the god (Neptune and his family), the clergy (none), the afterlife (Dutchman, the locker), sin (being cheap), ritual (the effigy), holidays (Christmas without Christ). Lighter and funnier. Its weakness is that the "dark twist" is mild.

**Angle C: "Mr. Krabs Is the Only Believer"**
Character study. Krabs is the one who prays (Patty Hype, and again in the spin-off), the one who gets judged (Born Again Krabs), the one who sells a soul, and the one who hears the church bell at the cemetery (Mid-Life Crustacean). The punchline is in Patty Hype: he prays "Great Neptune in Heaven" and seconds later swears "in the name of money". Tight and very on-brand, but it's narrower and probably runs short of twelve minutes without padding.
