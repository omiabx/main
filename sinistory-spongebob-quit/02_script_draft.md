## CHUNK 1 · COLD OPEN

[GFX: polaroid pinned on navy, handwritten caption "Welcome to the Chum Bucket (2002)"] [SFX: shutter]

**VO:** On January twenty-first, two thousand two, Mr. Krabs lost SpongeBob in a card game.

[CLIP chum 87.28–90.12 · KRABS: "I bet your contract, and I lost."]

**VO:** He'd been playing cards with Plankton every Thursday night for fifteen years and he never lost, so one night he got comfortable and put his fry cook in the pot. Plankton had been losing on purpose that entire time, waiting for exactly this.

So SpongeBob goes to work at the Chum Bucket, and Plankton tries everything. He builds him an exact replica of the Krusty Krab kitchen. He gets him fancy new shoes, a bubble bath with an ice cream cone, a toy seahorse to ride on and a bedtime story. At one point, according to Plankton, he even rubs his feet. SpongeBob still won't make a single patty.

[CLIP chum 536.70–539.00 · SPONGEBOB: "Tell you what, half-pint, why don't you ask me later?"]

**VO:** Eventually Plankton walks across the street and begs Krabs to take him back, and Krabs has one condition.

[CLIP chum 629.66–633.54 · KRABS: "How 'bout you give me fifty bucks, and I'll take him off your hands."]

**VO:** Plankton pays it. So Krabs gambled away his best employee and then got paid fifty dollars to take him back. SpongeBob walks into the Krusty Krab with a bandage on his head, and the first thing he does is make Krabs an offer.

[CLIP chum 663.16–667.48 · SPONGEBOB: "I'm workin' all day for free! My treat!" · KRABS: "That's what I thought you said."]

**VO:** So why doesn't he just quit? I went back through every episode where SpongeBob loses that job, and the strange part is that the show already answered the question, about twenty-five years ago. The answer ends with two skeletons. We'll get there.

## CHUNK 2 · PART 1: MAY I TAKE YOUR ORDER?

[GFX: cream card, "PART 1" typewriter, Anton "MAY I TAKE YOUR ORDER?", red underline] [SFX: typewriter, stamp]

**VO:** SpongeBob didn't fall into this job by accident. In the very first episode, which aired May first, nineteen ninety-nine, he's standing outside the Krusty Krab staring at a Help Wanted sign like somebody just proposed to him.

[CLIP helpwanted 151.48–153.26 · SPONGEBOB: "For years I've been dreaming of this moment."]

**VO:** Then he almost talks himself out of going in, because he's convinced he isn't good enough. Patrick has to give him a pep talk, and the first piece of evidence Patrick brings up is from when SpongeBob was a baby.

[CLIP helpwanted 173.82–177.18 · PATRICK: "Whose first words were 'May I take your order?'" · SPONGEBOB: "Mine were."]

**VO:** So the show tells you in episode one that before SpongeBob could say "Mom," he could take your order. Hang on to that, because Mr. Krabs clearly did.

## CHUNK 3 · PART 2: THE PAYCHECK

[GFX: cream card "PART 2 · THE PAYCHECK"] [SFX: typewriter, stamp]

**VO:** So what does the most devoted fry cook in the ocean actually get paid? The show never gives a number, but it drops a lot of hints, and none of them are good.

In Squid on Strike, it's payday. Squidward sniffs his envelope like it's a fine wine. Then Krabs holds out SpongeBob's envelope, and SpongeBob turns it down.

[CLIP strike 76.64–81.40 · SPONGEBOB: "I can't accept your money, Mr. Krabs." … "Grilling is my passion."]

**VO:** Krabs takes the envelope back without saying a word. Then he announces that from now on, he's charging them every time they goof off. Squidward gets an itemized bill that charges him a dollar for breathing and two for existing. SpongeBob doesn't argue. He pulls out a wad of cash and pays on the spot.

[GFX: punch-in on Squidward's bill, red marker circle on "Existing … 2.00"] [SFX: pop]

[CLIP strike 98.04–106.88 · SPONGEBOB: "…and twenty. Here ya go, Mr. K. I think this should cover all my nonsense. Oh, and here's an extra fifty cents for when I was tying my shoe."]

**VO:** He paid his boss for the seconds he spent tying his shoe.

Five years later, in Bummer Vacation, Krabs gets out of paying a union fine and holds up the money he saved. It's one coin, and SpongeBob is honestly impressed.

[CLIP bummer 636.40–642.66 · SPONGEBOB: "Wow! That's more than I make in a year!" · KRABS: "Don't be askin' me for a raise. Just get back to work."]

**VO:** As for raises, there's an official policy. The Krusty Krab Training Video has a whole section about it called "Interfacing With Your Boss," and the title card for that section is printed over an electric chair, with leather straps on the arms and a cord plugged into the wall.

[CLIP training 414.24–415.58 · title card, slow push-in] [SFX: hit]
[CLIP training 417.46–421.76 · SPONGEBOB: "Mr. Krabs, can I have a raise?" · KRABS: "No." · NARRATOR: "Good job, Mr. SquarePants!"]

**VO:** Krabs says no without looking up from his paperwork, and the narrator congratulates SpongeBob on taking it so well.

Then there's the time somebody asked Krabs directly what SpongeBob is worth. In Born Again Krabs, the Flying Dutchman offers him a trade: he can keep SpongeBob, or he can have whatever money the Dutchman has in his pocket. Seconds earlier, SpongeBob had bet his own soul that Krabs would never sell him out. Krabs has one question.

[CLIP bornagain 584.54–591.28 · KRABS: "That depends. How much money are we talking about?" · SPONGEBOB: "Mr. Krabs?" · DUTCHMAN: "Sixty-two cents." · KRABS: "I'll take the money."]

**VO:** When Squidward asks how he could trade SpongeBob for sixty-two cents, Krabs has exactly one follow-up.

[CLIP bornagain 613.02–614.20 · KRABS: "You think I could've gotten more?"]

## CHUNK 5 · SPONGEBOB MAKES THE NEWS

[GFX: newspaper-clipping card, typewriter "NEW YORK POST · OCT. 30, 2013", quote in Anton] [SFX: record scratch]

**VO:** That firing episode actually made the news. Before it even aired in the U.S., the New York Post praised SpongeBob for not, quote, "mooching off the social services of Bikini Bottom." Fox and Friends picked up the line and Al Sharpton answered back on MSNBC. For a little while, grown adults were arguing on television about a sponge's position on food stamps. Everyone fought about Patrick's speech. Nobody seemed bothered that the happy ending was SpongeBob running back to a man who fired him over a nickel.

## CHUNK 6 · PART 4: THE PASSION TAX

[GFX: cream card "PART 4 · THE PASSION TAX"] [SFX: typewriter, stamp]

**VO:** There's actual research on this. In twenty twenty, four researchers published a paper in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology called "Understanding contemporary forms of exploitation." Over a series of studies, they found that people think it's more acceptable to make a worker put in unpaid overtime, or do degrading jobs that aren't part of their role, as long as that worker seems passionate about what they do.

They also worked out how people justify it. The first excuse is that a passionate worker would have volunteered anyway. The second is that for someone who loves the job, the work is its own reward.

[GFX: two cream slips on navy: "WOULD HAVE VOLUNTEERED" · "THE WORK IS ITS OWN REWARD"] [SFX: pop, pop]

**VO:** Now listen to SpongeBob.

[CLIP chum 663.16–665.10 · SPONGEBOB: "I'm workin' all day for free! My treat!"] [GFX: red check on slip 1] [SFX: stamp]
[CLIP strike 76.64–81.40 · SPONGEBOB: "I can't accept your money, Mr. Krabs." … "Grilling is my passion."] [GFX: red check on slip 2] [SFX: stamp]

**VO:** SpongeBob makes both excuses himself before Krabs can.

The same researchers found it runs the other way too. When people see a worker being taken advantage of, they're more likely to assume that worker must really love the job.

And Krabs has always known what he's got. When SpongeBob walked out in Nature Pants, Krabs didn't offer him a raise or a day off. He looked at the hat on the ground and said "he'll be back," and he was right.

## CHUNK 7 · PART 5: ONE ETERNITY LATER

[GFX: cream card "PART 5 · ONE ETERNITY LATER"] [SFX: typewriter, stamp]

**VO:** Which brings us back to Squid on Strike, from two thousand one, the episode I think answered the question before anybody asked it.

After the bill, Squidward pulls SpongeBob aside and gives him a speech.

[CLIP strike 120.42–125.72 · SQUIDWARD: "We've got to unite as workers and demand the respect we deserve from the boss!"]

**VO:** SpongeBob is thrilled.

[CLIP strike 150.40–151.70 · SPONGEBOB (singing): "I still don't know what strike means."]

**VO:** They tell Krabs about the strike, and a second later they're both standing outside, fired, with SpongeBob's face pressed against the glass door.

[CLIP strike 176.96–182.08 · SPONGEBOB: "You didn't tell me I was gonna get fired! Without the Krusty Krab, I… I…"]

[ARCHIVAL rail1946 54.04–62.20 · NEWSREEL NARRATOR: "The wheels of the country's railroads grind slowly to a halt, as two hundred fifty thousand trainmen and engineers walk off the job."] [GFX: lower-left tag "UNIVERSAL NEWSREEL · MAY 23, 1946"]

[CLIP strike 549.22–551.62 · KRABS: "I'm ruined without you and the little yellow guy."]

[CLIP strike 604.68–608.22 · SPONGEBOB: "I will restore the working man to his rightful glory."]
