## The script (as spoken)

`[CLIP]` = a SpongeBob clip plays with its own (music-stripped) audio. Everything else is VO.

### 0:00 · "No, not really"
`[CLIP 01 · Mermaid Man Begins]` BARNACLE BOY: "It feels just like yesterday, doesn't it, Mermaid Man?" MERMAID MAN: "No, not really."

**VO 01**
> In 2011, SpongeBob aired an episode where Mermaid Man sits down to tell the story of how he became Mermaid Man. He gets as far as his washing machine. Then he falls asleep. That's kind of his whole deal. He's the oldest superhero in the ocean, and he can't remember his own past.
>
> Except that same episode answers the question anyway. It slips the answer in as two first names, and if you follow those two names, they take you to a TV show that went off the air in 1966.

### 0:30 · Shady Shoals
**VO 02**
> Quick refresher. Mermaid Man and Barnacle Boy show up in season one, August 1999. SpongeBob and Patrick are obsessed with their old TV show, so they go looking for the real guys. And they find them at Shady Shoals. Which is a retirement home.

`[CLIP 02 · S1]` SPONGEBOB: "Are you Mermaid Man and Barnacle Boy?" BB: "Well, we used to be, but now we're retired."

**VO 03**
> Within about a minute, you learn the two things that don't change about these guys for the next thirteen years. Mermaid Man cannot handle one specific word.

`[CLIP 03 · S1]` "There's evil afoot." / "EVIL!"

**VO 04**
> And Barnacle Boy is an old man who still gets treated like he's eight.

`[CLIP 04 · S1]` MM: "Make sure you give extra broccoli to my young ward. A boy needs his vitamins." … BB: "Will you stop calling me boy?"

### 1:15 · Who are you?
**VO 05**
> The memory thing is the running gag. At the end of that very first episode, they win, Barnacle Boy turns to the man he's been fighting crime with for decades, and says...

`[CLIP 05 · S1]` BB: "We did it, you old coot." MM: "Who are you?"

**VO 06**
> And the show never lets it go. In Back to the Past, he proudly shows SpongeBob and Patrick the cabinet where he keeps everything from his career.

`[CLIP 06 · Back to the Past]` MM: "This is our locker of memories. It contains... I can't remember."

### 1:45 · The washing machine
**VO 07**
> So in 2011 we get Mermaid Man Begins. A storm knocks out every TV in Bikini Bottom right before a never-before-aired Origin Special, and SpongeBob decides they'll go straight to the source and just ask him. Mermaid Man starts with the creation of the ocean. Barnacle Boy has to stop him. Then he tries again.

`[CLIP 07 · Mermaid Man Begins]` MM: "It all started when I was just a young, handsome, muscular lad... when suddenly I noticed that my washing machine had stopped." BB: "What are you talking about?"

**VO 08**
> He does get a version out eventually. Mermaids save him from drowning and give him a magic sea star. Later, he uses brand new telekinetic powers to swap a drowning kid's lungs for barnacles so the kid can breathe. That kid is Barnacle Boy. And Barnacle Boy, sitting right there, sixty-something years into this partnership, adds one detail.

`[CLIP 08 · Mermaid Man Begins]` BB: "The reason I teamed up with this joker is because I was stuck breathing underwater for the rest of my life."

### 2:40 · Ernie and Tim
**VO 09**
> Then the power comes back, and they all watch the official version. And I'm being completely serious when I say it's the dumbest origin story ever animated. There's a radioactive ant, a vat of acid, an alien spaceship, a solar eclipse on a leap year, a neon plaid lightning bolt, and the thing that finally gives them powers is popcorn that got left in the microwave too long.
>
> But go back to the first ten seconds of it. Before any of that happens, they're two regular guys in an apartment, and they call each other by name.

`[CLIP 09 · Mermaid Man Begins]` "Come on, Ernie, the movie's about to start. Where's the popcorn?" "It's in the microwave, Tim."

**VO 10**
> Ernie and Tim.
>
> Mermaid Man was voiced by Ernest Borgnine. Barnacle Boy was voiced by Tim Conway. Twelve years into the show, when they finally gave these two secret identities, they just used the actors' first names. And that makes a lot more sense once you know what those two actors were doing in 1962.

### 3:35 · PT-73
**VO 11**
> October 1962. ABC premieres McHale's Navy. It's a World War Two sitcom about the crew of one PT boat, number 73. Ernest Borgnine plays the skipper, Lieutenant Commander Quinton McHale. He's big, he's loud, and he's already got an Oscar at home. He won it in 1956 for a movie called Marty.
>
> And McHale's second in command is a skinny, clumsy ensign named Parker, played by a guy almost nobody had heard of yet. Tim Conway.
>
> The show ran a hundred and thirty-eight episodes, and ended in 1966. Conway went on to win Emmys on The Carol Burnett Show. And the two of them didn't do another TV show together for thirty-three years.

### 4:25 · The call
**VO 12**
> Then, in the late nineties, a marine biology teacher turned cartoonist named Stephen Hillenburg is putting together a show about a sponge. He and his creative director, Derek Drymon, are McHale's Navy fans. According to the show's casting supervisor, they wanted these two before casting had even started. They went to them directly, and both of them said yes.
>
> So in 1999, the skipper and his ensign are back on the same show. Borgnine is eighty-two. Conway is sixty-five. And the pecking order is exactly where they left it.

`[CLIP 10 · S1 opening]` NARRATOR: "Mermaid Man... with his young associate, Barnacle Boy..."

### 5:00 · Sixty-eight
**VO 13**
> Think about that from Tim Conway's side. In 1962 he's the junior officer taking orders from Ernest Borgnine. In 1999 he's the sidekick taking orders from Ernest Borgnine, and the character's name is Boy. And in 2002, the show gives Barnacle Boy an episode where he finally loses it.

`[CLIP 11 · MMBB V]` MM: "Krabby Patty for me and the Pip Squeak Patty for the boy." … BB: "I'm sixty-eight years old and I want a Krabby Patty!" … "I'm tired of playing second banana to a man who wears a bra!"

**VO 14**
> Sixty-eight. That episode aired in June 2002, and in June 2002, Tim Conway was sixty-eight years old. I can't prove that was on purpose. It is a very specific number to pick, though.
>
> He quits, joins the villains, gets most of what he asked for, and comes back. And here's the very last thing Mermaid Man says to him when he does.

`[CLIP 12 · MMBB V]` MM: "Good to have you back on the side of justice, Kyle."

**VO 15**
> Kyle. In 2002, Barnacle Boy's name is Kyle. Nine years later, it's Tim. So Mermaid Man isn't the only one with memory problems. The show forgot Barnacle Boy's name too.

### 6:00 · EVIL
**VO 16**
> Back to Borgnine for a second, because the most famous thing Mermaid Man ever does was an accident. While he was recording, his voice cracked on the word evil. They kept it. Here's how Borgnine told it later: "By golly! We started something, and we kept it in."
>
> In a 2011 interview, he said, "The kids scream and holler when I do that." This is a man who won an Academy Award in the fifties, and in his nineties, children knew him for one broken syllable.

`[CLIP 13 · MMBB V]` "Evil!" / "EVIL!"

### 6:35 · Batman and Robin
**VO 17**
> And the show kept doing this. In 2010, Back to the Past needed younger versions of the heroes, back in their prime. They cast Adam West and Burt Ward. Batman and Robin, from the 1966 Batman show.
>
> So you get a scene where the old heroes are stranded in the past, and their younger selves, voiced by TV's Batman and Robin, walk up to them, voiced by McHale and Parker, and have no idea who they are.

`[CLIP 14 · Back to the Past]` YOUNG MM: "Mermaid Man and Barnacle Boy are always here to help the elderly." … "There's something familiar about these two, Barnacle Boy. I just can't put my flipper on it." … "Let's get these poor, defenseless seniors out of harm's way."

**VO 18**
> Two duos from sixties TV in the same shot, and one of them doesn't recognize the other. I honestly don't think the show ever summed these characters up better than that.

### 7:15 · July 8th, 2012
**VO 19**
> July 8th, 2012. Ernest Borgnine dies at ninety-five. Nickelodeon runs a two-hour marathon of his episodes.
>
> That October, an episode called Patrick-Man airs. Patrick decides he wants to be a superhero after he sees an ad on TV. It's an ad for Mermaid Man and Barnacle Boy.

`[CLIP 15 · Patrick-Man!]` BB: "All in a day's work, eh, Mermaid Man?" MM: "Remember, it's up to you to reveal the true face of crime."

**VO 20**
> That's their last regular appearance. A commercial, playing on Patrick's TV, in somebody else's episode.
>
> And behind the scenes, Stephen Hillenburg had made a decision. He wouldn't recast Mermaid Man. Showrunner Vincent Waller later put it like this: "Steve was very clear he did not want to replace Ernest Borgnine with a sound alike. It was his call."
>
> The characters didn't disappear. They still turn up in the background, in cameos. They just don't talk anymore.
>
> In 2018, Waller was asked if Tim Conway would ever come back as Barnacle Boy. He said no, sadly, Mr. Conway was having health issues. Two months later, Stephen Hillenburg died. And in May 2019, Tim Conway died too. He was eighty-five.

### 8:10 · Yesterday
**VO 21**
> Every time Mermaid Man forgets something, it plays as a joke about an old man. But the show remembered all of it. It knew who these two were in 1962. It gave them a retirement home, and their real first names, and when they were gone, it didn't hand their voices to anybody else.
>
> Which brings me back to the first thing you heard in this video.

`[CLIP 16 · Mermaid Man Begins]` BB: "It feels just like yesterday, doesn't it, Mermaid Man?" MM: "No, not really."

**VO 22**
> Fair enough. For those two, yesterday was 1962.
